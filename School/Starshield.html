Key laws to memorize

Your three layers are exactly the right structure. They follow the order international law itself uses: whose act, then was a rule broken, then what follows and who decides. The laws below are sorted into those layers and ranked by importance.

Order of the argument (use this)

1Attribution: is it the state's act? ILC Arts. 4, 8 · Outer Space Treaty Art. VI
2Breach: did it break a rule that binds that state? ILC Art. 2 · Geneva Common Art. 1
3Consequences and forum: what follows, and who decides? ILC Arts. 30–31 · ICJ Statute Art. 36

Key findings for Starshield

Your main pointCutting aid isn't illegal intervention

The Court held the US aid cut, sugar-quota cut and embargo didn't breach non-intervention. The embargo was unlawful only because a 1956 treaty bound the US. The 2024 agreement's annex creates no obligations, so scaling back help breaks nothing.

Your backupPaying isn't controlling

Effective control (para. 115) is a high bar. Without proof of a specific US order, SpaceX's choices aren't America's under the general rules. (Outer Space Treaty Art. VI still applies in space.)

Thomas will useThe Geneva duty binds the US

Para. 220: the US must not encourage violations, and it did, with a CIA manual. Your answer: encouraging the violator is different from withholding help from the victim.

Layer 3Even winning got nothing

The US ignored the ruling, vetoed enforcement, and withdrew its consent to the Court. Nicaragua was never paid.

04

The clearly illegal case

Professor Byers told the class he'd seen Russian officials' business cards with Starlink contact details on the back. He then asked: what if the US were secretly giving Russia Starlink service? This is the mirror image of your case, and it shows exactly where the legal line is.

Reality check before the hypothetical

What SpaceX says

Starlink is inactive in Russia, and SpaceX “does not do business of any kind” with Russia's government or military (February 2024).

What Ukraine found

Russian units used smuggled terminals in occupied areas (2024). A whitelist and speed cap shut most of that down in February 2026.

What it doesn't show

Starlink is internet access, not an email service. Russians using it is not proof the US supplies it. The hypothetical assumes the US does so deliberately and secretly.

The line: withholding vs. giving

REAL: THE CAP

Less help to the victim
  • Held back help from Ukraine
  • No clear rule broken
  • Contested at best (Common Art. 1)
  • No criminal exposure
VS

HYPOTHETICAL: SECRET SUPPLY

Real help to the aggressor
  • Gives help to Russia
  • Several rules broken
  • Strong, if the help is significant and knowing
  • Possible ICC exposure for individuals; domestic risk if hidden from Congress

Run it through your three layers

Layer 1Whose act? Usually clear

US agencies doing it: the state's act (Art. 4). US secretly directing SpaceX: still the state's (Art. 8). US just tolerating it: not its act, but a failure to supervise (OST Art. VI).

Layer 2Rules broken: several

Complicity (ILC Art. 16); helping maintain aggression (Art. 41(2)); the uncontested “don't help violators” part of Common Art. 1; space activity breaking international law (OST Art. III); bad faith toward Ukraine (VCLT Art. 26).

Layer 3Accountability: still hard

The World Court still needs consent. But the ICC changes: Ukraine has been a member since Jan 1, 2025, so it can look at crimes on Ukrainian soil by anyone. Domestically, hiding it from Congress would repeat the Iran-Contra pattern.

Courts and accountability

First, clear up the names: the “World Court” and the ICJ are the same court. The ICC is a different court. Both sit in The Hague, which is why people mix them up.

ICJ · INTERNATIONAL COURT OF JUSTICE

a.k.a. the World Court · Peace Palace, The Hague · since 1945

Who's on trial
States vs. states. Never individuals.
Needs
Consent of both states (Art. 36).
Outcome
A binding judgment for the parties (Art. 59): stop, repair, pay.
Enforcement
The UN Security Council (Charter Art. 94(2)), where the US has a veto.
Also
Advisory opinions on request of UN bodies (Charter Art. 96). Not binding.
US status
UN member, but withdrew its standing consent in 1985, after Nicaragua.

ICC · INTERNATIONAL CRIMINAL COURT

The Hague · since 2002 · Rome Statute

Who's on trial
Individuals, never states.
Crimes
Genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, aggression.
Reach
Crimes on a member's territory, or by a member's nationals.
Outcome
Prison sentences; reparations to victims.
Ukraine
Member since Jan 1, 2025 (accepted its reach back to 2013).
US status
Not a member; rejects ICC power over US nationals.

What can a responsible state be made to do?

1 · Stop the act (ILC Art. 30)
→
2 · Promise not to repeat (Art. 30)
→
3 · Repair: restore, compensate, apologize (Arts. 31, 34–37)

For Starshield, step 1 already happened on Oct 24. That's why “it's fixed” is a remedy point, not a defence.

Why it's so hard to hold the US to account, even when something is clearly illegal

Consent

International courts mostly need the state to agree to be sued. The US withdrew its standing consent in 1985.

The veto

World Court judgments are enforced by the Security Council, where the US can veto. It did exactly that in 1986.

No ICC membership

The US isn't an ICC member and rejects its power over US nationals, and it won't hand anyone over.

Sovereign immunity

The US government can be sued in its own courts only where Congress allows, and war and foreign claims are mostly excluded.

Power and politics

Who will force a superpower? Allies need it, and enforcement depends on states willing to pay a price.

Precedent

Nicaragua won, and the US simply ignored the judgment. Everyone remembers that.

Why domestic is the best route

Inspector general finds the facts (briefing by Nov 1)
→
Congress holds hearings, demands reports
→
Money and law change (budgets, new rules)
→
Lies or cover-ups become crimes (the Iran-Contra lesson)

Live example: in October 2026, bipartisan lawmakers said Trump's Russian diesel deal violates the Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act. Domestic law is where the US actually gets checked.
