STARSHIELD STUDY

Was the October 2026 Starshield cap illegal?

From October 14 to 24, 2026, the military satellite service that guides Ukraine's drones, Starshield, dropped its connection for anything moving faster than about 161 km/h. Who ordered it is still disputed. Our position: the United States can probably be treated as responsible for the act, but no rule of international law clearly required it to keep the service at full speed, and no court can make it answer even if one did.

Our thesis
Unfriendly, not unlawful.
LAYER IWho did it?Was the cap the act of the United States?
Answer: probably yes. We concede this.
LAYER IIWas a rule broken?Did the act breach an obligation binding the US?
Answer: not clearly. We win here.
LAYER IIICan anyone hold the US to account?Which court or body could decide, and what follows?
Answer: no court. Only domestic routes.

I.1The method: three layers, in order

This is how international law itself analyzes responsibility, and it's the structure of our presentation. Each layer has its own question and its own rules. A case must win every layer to succeed.

I.2Which law outranks which

Read this first. The ladder below ranks legal authority: if two rules conflict, the higher one wins. It is not a ranking of what matters most in our case. Almost every rule our case turns on sits at Level 3. The top levels matter because they define the background: Russia's war is an act of aggression, and the UN Charter's rules on force and self-defence override other treaties.

What the investigations concluded. Congress's majority report (1987) said the President bore ultimate responsibility: if he didn't know what his advisers were doing, he should have. The independent counsel (1994) found no credible evidence Reagan knew of the diversion, but said he created the conditions for the crimes. The charges were mostly for lying to Congress, obstruction and destroying documents, not for the policy itself.

What it means for Starshield

Layer I · helps the other sidePrivate hands don't hide a government decision

Officials ran the policy through a private network. The same question arises here: did officials decide, with SpaceX as the hands? (ILC Art. 8)

Layer II · our pointIt was illegal because a law banned it

The Boland Amendment expressly forbade the funding. No law or treaty forbids slowing Starshield. Iran-Contra shows whose act it was, not that it was unlawful.

Layer I · oversight“Should have known”

Congress's finding mirrors the Outer Space Treaty's duty of continuing supervision: a government answers for what it should be watching.

Layer III · our pointAccountability was domestic, and limited

Hearings, a special prosecutor and trials, but convictions were overturned and pardons followed. That's the realistic ceiling for Starshield too.

In one sentence: Nicaragua sued the US at the World Court over its support for the Contras and its attacks on Nicaraguan ports; it won on most points, and the US ignored the judgment. It is the international law story of the same events.

What it means for Starshield

Layer II · our main authorityCutting aid isn't unlawful intervention

Ending aid, cutting a sugar quota and even a full trade embargo didn't breach the rule against intervention. Only a specific treaty made the embargo unlawful. The 2024 agreement disclaims any such obligation. (Nicaragua, aid findings)

Layer I · our backupPaying isn't controlling

Funding, arming and training weren't “effective control.” Being SpaceX's customer doesn't make its choices America's, unless there was a specific order. (para. 115; but see OST Art. VI)

Used by the other sideThe Geneva duty binds the US

The US had to avoid encouraging violations, and breached that with the CIA manual. Our distinction: that was encouraging the violator; the cap withheld help from the victim. (para. 220)

Layer III · our pointWinning wasn't enough

The US ignored the ruling, vetoed enforcement, and withdrew its consent to the Court. Nicaragua was never paid. (Charter Art. 94(2))

PART IV

The Professor's Hypothetical

The professor told the class he had seen Russian officials' business cards with Starlink contact details on the back, then asked: what if the US were secretly giving Russia Starlink service? This is the mirror image of the real case, and it shows exactly where the legal line sits.

Facts first

What SpaceX says

Starlink is inactive in Russia, and SpaceX does no business of any kind with Russia's government or military (February 2024).

What Ukraine found

Russian units used smuggled terminals in occupied areas (2024). A whitelist and speed caps shut most of that down in February 2026.

What the cards don't prove

Starlink is internet access, not an email service, so “Starlink email” likely means they connect through it. Russians using it doesn't show the US supplies it.

IV.1The line: withholding versus giving

The real case

The cap: less help to the victim

  1. The US held back a service from Ukraine.
  2. No rule clearly required the service.
  3. The only candidate (Common Art. 1) is contested.
  4. No criminal exposure.
VS
The hypothetical

Secret supply: real help to the aggressor

  1. The US would give a service to Russia.
  2. Several rules would be broken (below).
  3. Strong, if the help is significant and knowing.
  4. Possible ICC exposure for individuals; domestic risk if hidden from Congress.

IV.2Run it through the three layers

Layer IWhose act? Usually clear

US agencies supplying it: the state's own act (ILC Art. 4). SpaceX on secret US orders: still the state's (Art. 8). The US merely tolerating it: not its act, but a failure to supervise (OST Art. VI).

Layer IIRules broken: several

Complicity (ILC Art. 16); helping maintain aggression (Art. 41(2)); the accepted “don't help violators” part of Common Art. 1; space activity breaching international law (OST Art. III); bad faith toward Ukraine (VCLT Art. 26).

Layer IIIAccountability: still hard

The World Court still needs US consent. The ICC changes: Ukraine has been a member since January 1, 2025, so it can investigate crimes on Ukrainian soil by anyone (Rome Art. 25(3)(d)). Hiding it from Congress would break oversight law (National Security Act).

PART V

Courts and Accountability

The World Court and the ICJ are the same court. The ICC is a different court. Both sit in The Hague. One judges states; the other tries people.

INTERNATIONAL COURT OF JUSTICE

the “World Court” · Peace Palace · since 1945

Judges
States, never individuals.
Needs
Consent of the states (Statute Art. 36).
Produces
A binding judgment for the parties (Art. 59): stop, repair, compensate.
Enforced by
The UN Security Council (Charter Art. 94(2)), where the US has a veto.
Also
Advisory opinions at the request of UN bodies (Charter Art. 96).
The US
Withdrew its standing consent in 1985, after Nicaragua.

INTERNATIONAL CRIMINAL COURT

The Hague · since 2002 · the Rome Statute

Judges
Individuals, never states.
Crimes
Genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, aggression.
Reach
Crimes on a member's territory or by a member's nationals.
Produces
Prison sentences; reparations to victims.
Ukraine
Member since January 1, 2025.
The US
Not a member; rejects ICC jurisdiction over its nationals.

V.2What a responsible state can be made to do

1. Stop the act (ILC Art. 30)
→
2. Promise not to repeat it (Art. 30)
→
3. Repair: restore, compensate, acknowledge (Arts. 31, 34–37)

For Starshield, step 1 already happened on October 24. That's why “it's been fixed” is a remedy point, not a defence.

V.3Why the US is so hard to hold to account, even for a clear breach

Consent

International courts mostly need the state to agree. The US withdrew its standing consent to the World Court in 1985.

The veto

World Court judgments are enforced through the Security Council, where the US can veto. It did so after Nicaragua in 1986.

No ICC membership

The US isn't an ICC member, rejects its jurisdiction over Americans, and won't hand anyone over.

Sovereign immunity

The US can be sued in its own courts only where Congress allows; war and foreign claims are largely excluded.

Power and dependence

Enforcement needs states willing to pay a price. Ukraine depends on US support.

Precedent

Nicaragua won and was never paid. Everyone remembers that.

V.4Why the domestic route is the real one

Inspector general finds the facts
→
Congress holds hearings, demands reports
→
Budgets and laws change
→
Lies or cover-ups become crimes

A live example: bipartisan lawmakers say the October 9 Russian diesel deal violates the Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act. Domestic law is where the US actually gets checked.
